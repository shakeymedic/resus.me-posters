THE RULE OF SHOCK

Cognitive aid for the undifferentiated shocked patient. Oxygen delivery depends on more than just oxygen.

1. VOLUME

Is the tank empty?

  • Bleeding Chest, Abdo, Pelvis, Retroperitoneum, Long Bones, Floor.
  • Fluid Loss GI (V/D), Renal (DKA/DI), Skin (Burns).
  • Capillary Leak Sepsis, Anaphylaxis, Pancreatitis.
POCUS: Hyperdynamic LV, Flat IVC.

2. PUMP

Is the engine broken?

  • Myocardial Ischaemia, Infarction, Myocarditis, Contusion.
  • Rhythm Tachy (low filling), Brady (low output).
  • Valvular Acute regurgitation, Stenosis.
POCUS: Poor contractility, dilated LV/RV.

3. OBSTRUCTION

Is the pipe blocked?

1. TENSION

Pneumothorax

PPV converts simple PTX to tension.

Sign: Absent lung sliding (POCUS)

2. TAMPONADE

Cardiac

Beck's Triad is rare. Look for pulsus paradoxus.

Sign: RV diastolic collapse (POCUS)

3. THROMBUS

Pulmonary Embolism

Consider in sudden arrest with PEA. Hypoxia + Clear Chest.

Sign: Dilated RV, D-sign (POCUS)

SYNCOPE?

CHECK THE ECG FOR THE W.O.B.B.L.E.R.

In the well patient with syncope, a normal 12-lead ECG is reassuring. But subtle signs can predict sudden cardiac death.

W

WPW

Wolff-Parkinson-White

Short PR (<120ms) + Delta wave. Risk of SVT degenerating to VF.

O

OBSTRUCTED

AV Pathway Obstruction

1st degree block (PR >200ms)? Mobitz? Axis deviation suggests conduction disease.

B

BIFASCICULAR

Bifascicular Block

RBBB + LAFB (Left Axis) OR RBBB + LPFB (Right Axis). High risk for CHB.

B

BRUGADA

Sodium Channelopathy

V1-V2: Coved ST elevation >2mm (Type 1). Saddleback (Type 2).

L

LVH

Left Ventricular Hypertrophy

S in V1 + R in V5/6 > 35mm. Suggests HOCM (deep Qs) or Stenosis.

E

EPSILON

ARVC

Epsilon wave (notch) end of QRS in V1-V3. T-wave inversion V1-V3.

R

REPOLARISATION

QT Abnormalities

Long QT (QTc >450ms) → Torsades. Short QT (<330ms). R-on-T.

IF THE PATIENT LOOKS WELL BUT THE ECG WOBBLES, ADMIT FOR TELEMETRY.

THE HOT DEBRIEF FRAMEWORK

TIMING

Within 10 mins of event

S

SELF

"I felt..."

  • How was my cognitive bandwidth?
  • Did I feel overwhelmed? (Stress response)
  • Did I speak up when I had a concern?
T

TEAM

"We worked..."

  • Was the Team Leader clearly identified & hands-off?
  • Did we maintain a shared mental model?
  • Was communication closed-loop? ("Order → Task done")
E

ENVIRONMENT

"The room..."

  • Was the kit where we needed it? (Ergonomics)
  • Did equipment fail?
  • Was noise and crowd control managed effectively?
P

PATIENT

"The case..."

  • Specific clinical difficulties (e.g., distorted anatomy)?
  • Was the physiology unpredicted?
  • Did we miss a clinical cue?
S

SYSTEM

"The organization..."

  • Are there latent errors waiting to happen again?
  • Do we need to update a protocol or checklist?
  • Is there a training gap to address?

Kill the "Whimperative"

"Mitigated speech" is a disaster in resuscitation. It creates ambiguity when clarity is vital. Be polite, but be direct.

PASSIVE

"Would someone mind maybe grabbing the airway trolley?"

Ambiguous Target

"Any chance we could get a line in?"

Question vs Order

"I think we should probably shock him soon."

Hinting vs Action

DIRECT

"Steve, bring the airway trolley to the bedside."

Name + Action + Location

"Sarah, insert a large-bore IV in the left ACF."

Name + Specific Task

"Charge the defibrillator. Everyone stand clear."

Clear Command

Graded Assertiveness (PACE)

PROBE
"Do you know the sat is 88%?"
ALERT
"I am concerned about the sats."
CHALLENGE
"Stop. We need to bag him now."
EMERGENCY
"Take the blade. I am intubating."

CLINICAL TRAJECTORY

When resuscitation fails, we often default to "working harder" at the bottom of the pyramid. Stop. Move up a level.

WHY?

GOAL

What is the ultimate objective?

Fixation Error: "Intubating" vs "Oxygenating"
HOW?

STRATEGY

The plan to achieve the goal.

"RSI vs Awake Fibreoptic vs Surgical"
WHAT?

LOGISTICS

Specific procedures, drugs, position.

Kit selection Drug Dosing Position Lighting
WHO?

SKILLS

Technical competency & Psychomotor skills.

Dexterity Strength Familiarity

The Failure Trap

We tend to focus on the bottom layer (Skills). If you fail, don't just try harder at the skill. Move UP a level. Is the logistics (position) wrong? Is the strategy (plan) wrong?

The Zero Point Survey

30 seconds of preparation before the patient arrives is worth 30 minutes of chaos afterwards.

S

SELF

"I am calm. I am ready."

  • Cognitive unloading (Step back)
  • PPE check (Safety first)
  • Stress control breathing (Box breathing)
T

TEAM

"I am Team Leader."

  • Identify Team Leader (Don/Vest on)
  • Assign roles (Airway, Access, Drugs)
  • Stickers on / Introductions made
E

ENVIRONMENT

"Is the room ready?"

  • Trolley height set & brakes on
  • Airway plan (Plan A, B, C kit out)
  • Ultrasound machine on & gel ready
  • Crowd control (Clear the room)
P

PATIENT

"What do we know?"

  • ATMIST Summary review
  • Age / Weight (Broselow/WETFLAG)
  • Specific hazards (Chem/Rad/Bio)
U

UPDATE

"Here is the plan..."

  • Shared Mental Model Briefing
  • "We are expecting X"
  • "If X happens, we will do Y"
P

PRIORITIES

"The first action is..."

  • Immediate IV access?
  • Finger thoracostomy?
  • Transfer to CT (Don't take off scoop)?
